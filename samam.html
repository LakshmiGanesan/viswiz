A guide for the curious

Why “Samam”?

A tāḷam is a rhythmic cycle kept with the hand. However far the music wanders, it returns to the first count, the samam. The wheel at the centre is one full cycle, an āvartanam.

Three choices make every rhythm

Tāḷa family, the rows: seven patterns built from the laghu ǀ (clap and finger counts), drutam O (clap and wave) and anudrutam U (one clap).

Jāti, the columns: how many counts the laghu holds, 3, 4, 5, 7 or 9. Together they make 35 tāḷas.

Nadai, the row beneath: how many pulses fill each count. Five nadais take the 35 to 175 rhythms.

How to explore

Tap a number in the matrix to hear that tāḷa, and follow the wheel and the line of counts beneath it. Choose Hands for claps and waves, or Konnakol to hear the syllables recited. Laya sets the speed. The Chāpu tāḷas sit under “Beyond the 35”.
