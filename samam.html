A guide for the curious

Why “Samam”?

Watch a Carnatic singer and you will see a hand moving quietly on her lap: a clap, a ripple of fingers, a turn of the palm, and then the same again. That hand is keeping tāḷam, and however far the music wanders, it keeps returning to one place, the first count of the cycle. That count is the samam. This explorer is named after it.

What is tāḷam?

Tāḷam is the framework that organises musical time. If rāgam gives a composition its melodic shape, tāḷam gives it a rhythmic one. A tāḷa is a pattern of units measured in basic counts, or aksharas. One complete turn of the pattern is an āvartanam, the wheel at the centre of this page.

Three choices make every rhythm here

Tāḷa family (rows). Three aṅgas build every tāḷa: the laghu ǀ, a clap and finger counts of variable length; the drutam O, a clap and a wave, always two counts; and the anudrutam U, a single clap. Arranged in seven ways they give the Sulādi Sapta Tāḷas, from Dhruva to Eka.

Jāti (columns). The laghu can hold 3, 4, 5, 7 or 9 counts, each with its own syllables, or solkattu. Seven families in five jātis make 35 tāḷas, from 3 counts (Tisra Eka) to 29 (Saṅkīrṇa Dhruva). Ādi Tāḷam is Chaturasra Jāti Triputa, 4 + 2 + 2 = 8, and the page opens there.

Nadai (the row beneath). Jāti sets how long the cycle is; nadai sets how many pulses fill each count. Change it and the hands keep the same counts, only the rhythm inside them changes. 35 tāḷas in five nadais make 175. Speed, or laya, is a separate dial.

Beyond the 35

The Chāpu tāḷas are kept by beats rather than aṅgas: Khaṇḍa Chāpu (ta-ka · ta-ki-ta, 2 + 3), Miśra Chāpu (ta-ki-ta · ta-ka-di-mi, 3 + 4) and Viloma Chāpu, its reverse (ta-ka-di-mi · ta-ki-ta, 4 + 3). Rūpaka is also often kept as three beats in practice.

How to use it

Tap any number in the matrix to hear that tāḷa. Follow the wheel and the line of counts beneath it, and try keeping the tāḷam with your own hand. Switch the sound to Konnakol to hear the cycle as spoken strokes. Somewhere in the second or third cycle the counting gives way to listening, and you find yourself waiting for samam.
